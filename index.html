HOW GOOGLE WORKS

Three phases. Crawl. Index. Rank. Understanding this is the key to everything.

PHASE 1: CRAWLING

Google sends bots ("crawlers") to visit your site. They follow every link, read your content, images, videos, metadata. Think of it like a robot walking through your arcade, examining every machine.

What Gets Crawled

  • All pages you want indexed (specified in robots.txt)
  • All links on those pages
  • Images and their alt text
  • Videos and metadata
  • Structured data (schema markup)

What Doesn't Get Crawled

  • Pages blocked in robots.txt
  • Pages behind login walls
  • Pages that load via JavaScript only
  • Duplicate content (Google crawls canonical version)
  • Extremely slow pages (crawlers have time limits)

PHASE 2: INDEXING

Google processes what it crawled and adds it to its index. Think of the index as Google's giant library: every page it knows about, catalogued and searchable.

What Triggers Indexing

  • Pages are crawlable (not blocked)
  • Pages have unique, original content
  • Pages don't violate Google policies
  • Page has been crawled at least once

PHASE 3: RANKING

When someone searches, Google retrieves indexed pages matching the query and ranks them using 200+ factors. Top 10 results = page 1. That's where you want to be.

THE RANKING FACTORS

Relevance (E-E-A-T)

Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Show you're the authority on your event.

Backlinks

Links from other sites are "votes" for your credibility. Quality over quantity.

Page Speed

Slow pages rank lower. Target under 3 seconds on mobile.

Mobile-Friendliness

Google indexes mobile version first. Non-mobile-friendly sites are penalised.

Content Quality

Longer, comprehensive content typically ranks better. But only if it's actually good.

User Signals

Click-through rate (CTR), bounce rate, time on page. Do users click and stay?

CORE WEB VITALS

Google heavily weighs three user experience metrics:

LCP - Largest Contentful Paint

How long main content takes to load. Target: under 2.5 seconds.

FID - First Input Delay

How responsive your site is to clicks/scrolls. Target: under 100ms.

CLS - Cumulative Layout Shift

How much page elements move around as it loads. Target: under 0.1.

Poor Core Web Vitals = lower rankings. Better metrics = better rankings, even with the same content.

SEARCH INTENT

Google understands not just the words, but the intent behind them.

Intent Types

Navigational: "Code DXB registration" - They want to register. Serve your registration page.

Informational: "What is AI executive networking" - They want to learn. Serve educational content.

Commercial: "Best AI conference Middle East" - They're comparing. Show why yours is best.

Local: "Exhibitions near me" - They want events in their location. Google shows local results.

Matching intent is critical. If you rank for a keyword but content doesn't match intent, users bounce, and Google demotes you.

ON-PAGE SEO ADVANCED

Every element on the page that tells Google what it's about.

TITLE TAGS & META DESCRIPTIONS

Your title tag is the link shown in search results. Your meta description is the snippet below it.

Bad vs Good Title Tags

Bad: "Home" (tells Google nothing)

Good: "Code DXB 2027: AI Executive Conference Dubai, April 1-2"

Good title: keyword at start, descriptive, under 60 chars, includes location.

LINK BUILDING MASTERY

How to earn high-quality backlinks that directly impact rankings and drive traffic.

WHY LINKS STILL MATTER

Backlinks are "votes" for your site. A link from a respected site signals authority to Google. Quality over quantity: one link from Forbes is worth 100 links from random blogs.

8 LINK BUILDING STRATEGIES FOR EVENTS

  • Press releases & media coverage (event announcements, speakers, milestones)
  • Industry directory listings (event directories, chamber of commerce)
  • Content that earns links (surveys, original research, expert roundups)
  • Outreach & guest articles (pitch to event blogs, industry publications)
  • Sponsorship links (sponsors link back to your site)
  • Broken link building (find broken links, suggest your content as replacement)
  • Resource page links (event organisers list other events)
  • Review & quote links (get quoted in articles, get linked)

FAQ & TROUBLESHOOTING

Answers to your hardest questions.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Q: How long until we see results?

A: 3-6 months minimum. Google needs time to crawl, index, and re-rank your site. Be patient. Paid ads are faster, SEO is longer-term.

Q: We're already ranking #1. Now what?

A: Maintain and expand. Keep publishing content. Target new keywords. Defend your position by staying ahead of competitors.

Q: What if our event is brand new?

A: Start with press releases and media coverage to build initial links and awareness. Then execute the 12-month plan. New events take longer to rank.

Q: Can we rank without a blog?

A: Difficult. A blog is your publishing engine. Without it, you're relying on static pages, which limits keyword opportunities. Build a blog.

Q: Should we focus on Google or other search engines?

A: Google first. It has 92% market share. Master Google. Bing, DuckDuckGo, and others will follow.

Q: What's the best keyword density?

A: There's no magic number. Avoid keyword stuffing. Use your target keyword naturally in the title, heading, and first paragraph. Then focus on user experience, not density.

Q: Do we need to update old blog posts?

A: Yes. Updating old content often boosts rankings faster than creating new content. Refresh with new data, add internal links, improve structure.

Q: How many backlinks do we need?

A: Quality over quantity. 10 links from authoritative sites beat 1,000 links from spam. Focus on earning links from relevant, high-authority domains.

Q: Should we buy links?

A: Never. Google penalises paid links. Build links the hard way: through great content, outreach, press coverage, and earning mentions.

Q: What about social media? Does it help SEO?

A: Indirectly. Social doesn't directly boost rankings, but it increases reach, engagement, and potential for backlinks. Use both.

TROUBLESHOOTING

Problem: We're not ranking for any keywords

Diagnosis: Site may not be indexed. Check Google Search Console for crawl errors. Check robots.txt. Fix technical issues first.

Problem: Rankings dropped suddenly

Diagnosis: Algorithm update or manual penalty. Check if you have penalty in Search Console. Audit for over-optimisation, duplicate content, or low-quality backlinks.

Problem: High traffic but low conversions

Diagnosis: Rank for wrong keywords (mismatch between search intent and page content). Audit keywords. Target more conversion-focused terms.

Problem: Competitors rank higher even though our content is better

Diagnosis: They have more backlinks and domain authority. Build your link profile. Get media coverage. Publish original research.
